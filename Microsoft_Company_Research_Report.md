# Microsoft Corporation (`MSFT`) — Company Research Report

**Valuation date:** September 1, 2026. *(UNSOURCED — supplied valuation date.)* **Recommendation:** Watch / defer. *(UNSOURCED — analyst judgment retained from prior report.)*

## Company at a glance

Microsoft is a technology company that earns revenue from cloud solutions and services, software licensing and support, online advertising, and devices and gaming products. *(Source: FY2026 10-K, Item 1, p. 3; Item 7, pp. 33–34.)* Its common stock trades under the symbol `MSFT`. *(Source: FY2026 10-K cover page.)*

The FY2026 annual report covers the year ended June 30, 2026 and was filed July 29, 2026 under accession `0001193125-26-323660`. *(Source: FY2026 10-K cover page; SEC filing index.)* Financial-statement values below are U.S. dollars in millions unless stated otherwise. *(Source: FY2026 10-K, Item 8, financial-statement table headings.)*

## What drove the year

Revenue increased $50.1 billion, or 18%, in FY2026, and management attributes the increase primarily to Microsoft Cloud growth, including Intelligent Cloud/Azure and Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud. *(Source: FY2026 10-K, Item 7, “Fiscal Year 2026 Compared with Fiscal Year 2025,” pp. 33–35.)* Microsoft Cloud revenue increased 27% to $214.4 billion, while Azure and other cloud-services revenue increased 41%. *(Source: FY2026 10-K, Item 7, “Overview,” p. 34.)*

**Analyst judgment:** Azure/cloud is the load-bearing growth driver; the valuation should not simply extend Microsoft’s consolidated 18% growth rate. *(UNSOURCED — analyst judgment retained from prior report.)*

Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud revenue increased 17%, Microsoft 365 Consumer cloud revenue increased 28%, LinkedIn revenue increased 11%, Dynamics 365 revenue increased 18%, search advertising excluding traffic-acquisition costs increased 12%, and Xbox content and services revenue declined 5%. *(Source: FY2026 10-K, Item 7, “Overview,” p. 34.)* This mixture means Microsoft retains material consumer, device, gaming, advertising, and on-premises exposure alongside its cloud business. *(Source: FY2026 10-K, Item 1, pp. 3–11; Item 7, pp. 33–41.)*

## Margin and investment lens

Cost of revenue increased 21%, and Microsoft Cloud gross margin declined to 66%. *(Source: FY2026 10-K, Item 7, “Fiscal Year 2026 Compared with Fiscal Year 2025,” pp. 34–35.)* Management attributes the gross-margin pressure to continued AI-infrastructure investment and growing AI-product usage, partly offset by efficiency gains in Microsoft Cloud. *(Source: FY2026 10-K, Item 7, “Fiscal Year 2026 Compared with Fiscal Year 2025,” p. 35.)*

**Analyst judgment:** AI is both a demand opportunity and a cost/capital-intensity issue; the central valuation question is whether incremental cloud revenue generates enough margin and free cash flow to justify the infrastructure buildout. *(UNSOURCED — analyst judgment retained from prior report.)*

## Liquidity and capital structure

Microsoft reported $20.935 billion of cash and cash equivalents and $55.908 billion of short-term investments, totaling $76.843 billion at June 30, 2026. *(Source: FY2026 10-K, Item 8, Consolidated Balance Sheets, p. 51.)* It reported $9.227 billion of current long-term debt and $31.067 billion of long-term debt, for $40.294 billion of stated debt carrying amount. *(Source: FY2026 10-K, Item 8, Consolidated Balance Sheets, p. 51.)*

For per-share analysis, the filing reports 7.453 billion diluted weighted-average shares for FY2026, comprising 7.429 billion basic weighted-average shares plus 24 million dilutive stock-based awards. *(Source: FY2026 10-K, Item 8, Note 2 — Earnings Per Share.)* This differs from the 7.426 billion shares outstanding reported as of July 23, 2026. *(Source: FY2026 10-K cover page.)*

## Risks and what to monitor

Management identifies intense competition, cybersecurity and privacy risks, global economic and geopolitical risks, and the need to adapt its products, business models, and sales motions. *(Source: FY2026 10-K, Item 1A — Risk Factors; Item 1, pp. 3–11.)* Commercial remaining performance obligation increased 84% to $678 billion. *(Source: FY2026 10-K, Item 7, “Overview,” p. 34.)*

**Analyst judgment:** The obligation balance needs analysis of duration, cancellation, and revenue-recognition timing; it is not equivalent to current revenue. *(UNSOURCED — analyst judgment retained from prior report.)*

**Analyst judgment:** The thesis weakens if cloud growth requires permanently elevated infrastructure spending, if competition compresses cloud economics, or if a security or regulatory event materially damages customer trust. *(UNSOURCED — analyst judgment retained from prior report.)*

## Research agenda

The next work should reconcile cloud growth, cloud gross margin, capital expenditures, and depreciation across future filings to distinguish a temporary AI-infrastructure burden from a structural one. *(UNSOURCED — research priority retained from prior report.)* It should model Azure/cloud, Microsoft 365, and consumer/device/gaming separately instead of applying a single consolidated growth rate. *(UNSOURCED — research priority retained from prior report.)* It should then develop a filing-reconciled FCFF valuation, documented WACC assumptions, an enterprise-to-equity bridge, and downside cases for slower cloud growth, lower margins, higher AI infrastructure investment, and a higher discount rate. *(UNSOURCED — research priority retained from prior report.)*

## Conclusion

**Watch / defer.** Microsoft’s operating momentum and liquidity justify deeper valuation work, but they do not by themselves establish that the shares are undervalued. *(UNSOURCED — analyst judgment retained from prior report.)* I would change this call only after a defensible intrinsic-value range shows a margin of safety that remains intact under reasonable downside cases. *(UNSOURCED — analyst judgment retained from prior report.)*
